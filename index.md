## About Me

I am a political sociologist working at the [Max Planck Institute for Political and Social Science Göttingen](https://www.ips.mpg.de/3004/en) in the Department of Inequality, Transformation and Conflict. Broadly speaking, my research focuses on how people develop their political attitudes based on media sources, biographical experiences, or current representations of societal transformations. To achieve this, I employ various data and methods, including longitudinal and cross-sectional surveys, experiments, text-as-data approaches to media content, qualitative content analysis, and group discussions.

My work has appeared in [European Societies](https://doi.org/10.1162/euso_a_00030), [Socius](https://doi.org/10.1177/23780231261476528), the [Cologne Journal of Sociology and Social Psychology](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11577-023-00894-3), and [Zeitschrift für Soziologie](https://doi.org/10.1515/zfsoz-2025-2015). Some of my research articles have also been covered by major German media outlets, including [Deutschlandfunk](https://www.deutschlandfunk.de/neue-studie-groesserer-demokratieverdruss-bei-anhaengern-des-ddr-regimes-100.html) and the [Frankfurter Allgemeine Zeitung](https://www.faz.net/aktuell/wissen/geist-soziales/nicht-alle-deutschen-nehmen-unterschiede-zwischen-ost-und-west-wahr-19709990.html).


## Research Topics
### Social Change & Transformations
The economic and social ruptures in Central and Eastern Europe after 1989 are becoming increasingly salient in public debates to explain the rise of right-wing populism, skepticism against democracy, and resistance to new social changes. This project aims to shed light on how economic turmoil, mass unemployment, shifting status hierarchies, and the transition to democracy in the 1990s have shaped people's attitudes, worldviews, and perceptions of society to this day. The case of East Germany, where the GDR quickly became part of the Federal Republic, should be understood in the context of other Central and Eastern European countries, such as Poland, the Czech Republic or Hungary.

## Climate Change, Media & Attitudes
Most people in Germany and Europe agree that climate change poses a significant threat. Nevertheless, debates about climate policies in the public sphere often appear polarized. In this project, I explore the role of the media in perceived polarization around climate policies in Germany. More specifically, my research examines how media content triggers people's moralities and how social figures can contribute to a better understanding of polarization in this context. Additionally, I am interested in how perceptions of social change drive opposition against climate measures.
